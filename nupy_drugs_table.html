Levodopa (L-DOPA) + carbidopaLevodopa = prodrug precursor of dopamine that crosses the BBB (dopamine itself cannot); converted by DOPA decarboxylase (DDC) centrally and peripherally. Carbidopa = peripheral DDC inhibitor that does not enter the CNS → less peripheral conversion, more levodopa reaches the brain, lower dose, fewer peripheral effects. Goal: restore striatal dopamine lost from substantia nigra neurons.1st line main drug to increase CNS dopamine in Parkinson disease; always co-administered with carbidopa (carbidopa/levodopa; also carbidopa + entacapone combination tablet). PK: peak 1–2 h, t½ 1–3 h; dietary amino acids compete for gut absorption and BBB transport → take the main protein meal in the evening.
  • Peripheral: nausea/vomiting (CTZ) — divided doses, slow titration, carbidopa; postural hypotension and falls (↓ peripheral catecholamines + D1 vasodilation); arrhythmia/tachycardia (rare, ↓ with carbidopa); brown urine/saliva/vaginal secretions (melanin)
  • Central: hallucinations, confusion (quetiapine is the least harmful antipsychotic if needed)
  • Dyskinesias + response fluctuations in up to 80 % after 10 years: choreoathetosis of face/distal limbs; wearing-off (effect fades hours after a dose — timing-related) and on-off (unpredictable akinetic 'off' periods alternating with mobile-but-dyskinetic 'on') → adjust dose/interval, long-acting forms, evening protein, add adjuncts (MAO-B, COMT, DA agonists, amantadine)
  • Interactions: pyridoxine (B6) ↑ peripheral metabolism; MAO-A inhibitors → hypertensive crisis (avoid within 2 weeks). Contraindicated in psychosis and narrow-angle glaucoma.
⭐ Carbidopa blocks peripheral DDC so levodopa reaches the brain; wearing-off vs on-off; B6 and MAO-A inhibitors are the interactions.

Opioid receptors & endogenous systemEndogenous agonists: endorphins, enkephalins, dynorphins, endomorphins. Three receptors — µ, δ, κ — each a separate gene (subtypes by splicing/heteromerization), all GPCRs coupled to Gi/Go: ↓ adenylyl cyclase (↓ cAMP), ↓ voltage-gated Ca²⁺ → ↓ transmitter release (glutamate, substance P) presynaptically; Gβγ opens GIRK K⁺ channels → hyperpolarization postsynaptically. µ = supraspinal + spinal analgesia, sedation, respiratory depression, slowed GI transit, hormone modulation; δ = analgesia + hormone modulation; κ = analgesia, psychotomimetic effects, slowed GI transit.µ is the primary analgesic target; analgesia = inhibition of the ascending pain pathway + activation of the descending inhibitory pathway.κ agonism explains dysphoria/psychotomimetic effects of mixed agonists.⭐ µ/δ/κ are Gi/Go GPCRs: ↓ cAMP, ↓ Ca²⁺ release, ↑ K⁺ hyperpolarization; µ carries analgesia AND respiratory depression.

Opioid toxicity triad · tolerance vs no tolerance · withdrawal · contraindications (high-yield unit)Overdose triad: respiratory depression + pinpoint miosis + CNS depression/coma (miosis persists because no tolerance develops). Tolerance = ↓ potency with prolonged exposure — HIGH for analgesia, euphoria, mental clouding, sedation, respiratory depression, antidiuresis, nausea/vomiting, cough suppression; MODERATE for bradycardia; MINIMAL/NONE for miosis and constipation. Cross-tolerance between opioids is variable → manage by opioid rotation.Reversal: naloxone (rapid, short 1–2 h — repeat/observe). Physical dependence = physiological adaptation; withdrawal syndrome from abrupt cessation OR an antagonist / partial agonist. Substance use disorder = craving, seeking, continued use despite harm. Prescribe short courses (3–7 days) for pain; long-term only for terminal cancer / opioid use disorder; chronic neuropathic and inflammatory pain → other drugs; never withhold pain relief.
  • Main adverse: dysphoric restlessness/tremulousness, respiratory depression, nausea/vomiting, ↑ intracranial pressure, postural hypotension (worse with hypovolaemia), constipation, urinary retention, nasal itching/urticaria
  • Contraindications/cautions: full + partial agonist or antagonist together (withdrawal); reduced pulmonary function (respiratory failure); head injury (CO₂ retention → cerebral vasodilation + ↑ ICP); pregnancy (fetal dependence; poor fetal BBB); hepatic/renal impairment (↑ levels, ↑ t½)
  • Interactions (high fatality risk): sedative-hypnotics/alcohol/benzodiazepines (respiratory depression), typical antipsychotics (sedation, CV effects), MAOIs → serotonin syndrome + sedation
⭐ Triad = respiratory depression + miosis + coma → naloxone; miosis and constipation never develop tolerance; head injury and pulmonary disease are the classic contraindications.
