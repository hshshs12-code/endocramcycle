Sleep-cycle architecture across a night (hypnogram)

  1. Sleep onsetAwake (beta / alpha) → stage 1 theta → stage 2 spindles + K-complexes.
  2. DescentStage 3 (20-50 % delta) → stage 4 (> 50 % delta) within the first hour.
  3. First REMThe record climbs back up and the first REM period appears at roughly 70 min on the figure.
  4. Repeating cyclesThe cycle repeats several times a night.
  5. Drift across the nightDeep stage 3-4 sleep is concentrated in the early cycles; REM periods get longer toward morning.
  6. Measured byPolysomnography: EEG + EOG/EMG + heart rate + respiration.

Meninges, Ventricles and CSF — production, flow, absorption, hydrocephalus· 12PROCESS card for the CSF circulation path, a fact table, then COMPARE-&-CONTRAST rows for the hydrocephalus types.

CSF circulation path

  1. 1 · Lateral ventriclesChoroid plexus secretes CSF.
  2. 2 · Interventricular foramina (Monro)Lateral → third ventricle.
  3. 3 · Third ventricleIts choroid plexus adds more CSF.
  4. 4 · Cerebral aqueduct (Sylvius)Third → fourth ventricle; no choroid plexus here.
  5. 5 · Fourth ventricleAdds more CSF; exits by the median aperture (Magendie) → cisterna magna and the lateral apertures (Luschka).
  6. 6 · Subarachnoid space + cisternsCSF bathes the external surfaces of brain and cord (80 % of volume).
  7. 7 · Arachnoid granulationsOne-way valves into the dural venous sinuses (CSF pressure > venous pressure).

Workshop: Muscle Weakness — neuromuscular junction and excitation-contraction physiology· 8PROCESS card for synaptic transmission at the NMJ (the only synapse the lectures walk through step by step), then a component table and a compare row.

Synaptic transmission at the neuromuscular junction

  1. 1 · Action potentialArrives at the presynaptic terminal of the motor neuron.
  2. 2 · Ca2+ entryVoltage-gated Ca2+ channels open; Ca2+ enters.
  3. 3 · ReleaseVesicles release ACh into the 20-30 nm cleft.
  4. 4 · BindingACh binds nicotinic receptors on the junctional folds.
  5. 5 · End-plate depolarisationChannels open → Na+ influx → muscle action potential.
  6. 6 · SpreadT-tubules carry it inward; SR releases Ca2+ → troponin → contraction.
  7. 7 · TerminationAcetylcholinesterase hydrolyses ACh to acetate + choline.
