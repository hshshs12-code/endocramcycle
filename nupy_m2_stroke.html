Acute ischemic stroke — algorithm (TIME is BRAIN)

  1. RecognizeSudden focal deficit; history (onset time, pattern F/A/L), vitals (BP, pulse rhythm), neuro exam
  2. 1. Noncontrast CTTop priority — rule out hemorrhage (thrombolytics are contraindicated in hemorrhagic stroke); normal CT does not rule out ischemia
  3. 2. Immediate labsCBC + platelets, PT/PTT/INR, cardiac markers, electrolytes, BUN/creatinine, glucose, lipids, O2 sat
  4. 3. ReperfusionNo bleed and within 3-4.5 h of last symptom-free → tPA protocol (restore flow to ischemic but not yet infarcted brain)
  5. 4. ThrombectomyMechanical thrombectomy (endovascular) — alternative, up to 24 h for large-vessel anterior circulation clots
  6. 5. Start therapyLow-dose aspirin + statin immediately once hemorrhage excluded (any degree of stenosis)
  7. 6. BPLower only if > 220/120; tPA candidates need < 185/110 (labetalol, nitroprusside, CCB)
  8. 7. Look for sourceCT/MR angiography, echocardiogram, carotid ultrasound, ECG (AF)

tPA — key contraindications

  1. BleedingActive bleeding anywhere, incl. hemorrhagic stroke
  2. ImagingMultilobar infarcts
  3. BP> 185/110 mm Hg if it cannot be adequately lowered (labetalol etc.) — e.g. 200/110 excludes
  4. Recent eventsHead trauma, prior stroke or MI within 3 months; GI bleed within 3 weeks; major surgery within 2 weeks
  5. CoagulationElevated INR or PTT (e.g., heparin or warfarin — heparin 24 h ago excludes); platelets < 100,000/mm3
  6. Not a contraindicationIschemic signs, negative CT, presentation within 3 h — these are the indications

Stroke — definition, types & epidemiologyNeurological deficit from injury/death of brain cells in a localized area when cerebral circulation is disrupted (abrupt vascular insufficiency or bleeding). Ischemic >80%: embolism, thrombosis (large vessel, lacunar), systemic hypoperfusion (watershed), TIA.
