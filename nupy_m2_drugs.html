Status epilepticus — treatment protocol

  1. RecognizeSeizure >5 min, or ≥2 seizures without regaining consciousness → emergency protocol
  2. Step 1IV benzodiazepine: lorazepam or diazepam (IM midazolam if IV access will be delayed)
  3. Step 2If the seizure continues: add phenytoin or fosphenytoin (preferred IV — water-soluble prodrug) or pentobarbital (second line)
  4. Long termBenzodiazepines are not used for chronic control (sedation, tolerance, dependence/abuse)

CONCEPT — How AEDs work (6 mechanisms)1) Voltage-gated Na⁺ channel block — binds the most rapidly cycling channels, keeps them closed longer → delays next AP → ↓ glutamate release. 2) T-type Ca²⁺ channel block (postsynaptic thalamic pacemaker). 3) Presynaptic P/Q-type Ca²⁺ channel block via α2δ subunit. 4) Enhance GABA-A — ↑ frequency or ↑ duration of Cl⁻ opening → hyperpolarization. 5) Inhibit GABA transaminase. 6) Inhibit SV2A. Also NMDA antagonist (felbamate), AMPA antagonist (topiramate).Na⁺: valproate, lamotrigine, topiramate (broad); carbamazepine, phenytoin/fosphenytoin, oxcarbazepine (narrow). T-type Ca²⁺: ethosuximide (absence only), valproic acid. α2δ: gabapentin. ↑ GABA-A frequency: topiramate, benzodiazepines; ↑ duration: phenobarbital, pentobarbital. GABA-T: valproic acid. SV2A: levetiracetam.Topiramate has two and valproate has three known mechanisms (valproate: GABA-T inhibition, T-type Ca²⁺ block, Na⁺ block). Benzodiazepines, barbiturates, valproic acid and topiramate all enhance GABA at GABA-A receptors.⭐ Absence seizures = thalamic T-type Ca²⁺ channels → ethosuximide/valproate; gabapentin = α2δ; levetiracetam = SV2A.

Dopamine pathways — what D2 blockade does

  1. MesolimbicVTA → limbic system (nucleus accumbens, amygdala). Over-active in psychosis → positive symptoms; site of therapeutic action.
  2. MesocorticalVTA → cortex. ↓ activity → negative symptoms; blockade can worsen them.
  3. NigrostriatalSubstantia nigra → striatum. Blockade → EPS (dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism); up-regulation → tardive dyskinesia.
  4. TuberoinfundibularHypothalamus → pituitary. DA inhibits prolactin → blockade → hyperprolactinemia.
  5. Serotonin & glutamate5-HT inhibits cortical DA release (why 5-HT2A antagonism helps); glutamate→GABA links raise mesolimbic DA (positive) and lower mesocortical DA (negative symptoms).

Addiction vocabulary

  1. AddictionCompulsive behavioral pattern of procuring and using the drug (psychological dependence, craving).
  2. ToleranceSmaller effect with the same dose — pharmacokinetic (enzyme induction), pharmacodynamic (receptor down-regulation), learned, cross-tolerance (alcohol ↔ BZD).
  3. Physical dependenceContinued use needed to prevent withdrawal.
  4. WithdrawalPhysical/psychological signs on abstinence — clear for alcohol, BZDs, opioids, nicotine.
  5. RelapseRenewed use after abstinence; cues trigger use; former tolerant dose may now be lethal.
  6. Risk reductionStrategies to reduce harm that need not require abstinence.

Acute ischemic stroke — management

  1. Time is brainGoal of reperfusion: restore flow to ischemic but not yet infarcted brain.
  2. 1. Noncontrast CTTop priority — rule out hemorrhage (blood white, ischemia dark; normal CT does not exclude ischemia). Labs: CBC/platelets, PT/PTT/INR, glucose, electrolytes, BUN/Cr, cardiac markers, lipids, O2 sat — don't delay tPA for labs unless a contraindication is likely.

  4. 3. Antiplatelet + statinLow-dose aspirin + statin once hemorrhage excluded.
  5. 4. BPTreat only if >220/120; <185/110 required for tPA (labetalol, nicardipine).
  6. 5. GeneralNormal saline (avoid free water/dextrose); treat hyperglycemia and fever (antipyretics ×48 h); correct hypoglycemia (mimics deficits) and hyponatremia.

Platelet plug formation — antiplatelet targets

  1. AdhesionGpIb binds subendothelial collagen via von Willebrand factor.
