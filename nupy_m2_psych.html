Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) for delirium

  1. 1 · Acute onset & fluctuating courseFrom family/nurse: acute change from baseline? Behaviour comes and goes during the day?
  2. 2 · InattentionEasily distractible, can't keep track of conversation
  3. 3 · Disorganized thinkingRambling, irrelevant, illogical, switching subjects
  4. 4 · Altered level of consciousnessAnything but 'alert': vigilant, lethargic, stupor, coma
  5. DiagnosisDelirium = features 1 + 2 + (3 or 4)

Work-up & management of acute confusion

  1. HistoryAcute? Altered consciousness? MOVESTUPID causes, new medications, intoxication/withdrawal, sleep-wake change, fluctuation, autonomic signs (sweating, tachycardia), PMH
  2. Re-examineRepeat the physical even if recently done; hunt for occult UTI and pneumonia (may lack fever/cough)
  3. InvestigationsCMP (glucose, Ca, electrolytes, LFTs, BUN, creatinine), CBC, UA + culture, toxicology screen, ethanol level, blood gases; ESR, Mg, ammonia, TSH/T3/T4, B12; chest x-ray, CT/MRI; LP last resort if meningitis suspected
  4. SafetyPad side rails, glove hands; restraints only as last resort
  5. Treat cause(s)No specific delirium treatment; there may be >1 cause (UTI + dehydration, pneumonia + DKA)
  6. Don't add toxicityAvoid sedatives, anxiolytics, antipsychotics

Why thiamine before glucose (Wernicke)

  1. Thiamine neededPyruvate dehydrogenase (pyruvate → acetyl-CoA) requires thiamine
  2. Glucose load↑ glucose metabolism consumes the remaining thiamine
  3. Lactic acidosisBlocked PDH → lactate accumulates (consider thiamine deficiency in unexplained lactic acidosis)
  4. Focal damageMammillary bodies & posteromedial thalamus; NMDA-mediated apoptotic cell death
  5. RuleGive IV thiamine first, then glucose; never delay for tests or imaging
